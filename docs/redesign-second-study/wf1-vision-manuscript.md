# THE LIVING HAND

*"This living hand, now warm and capable — see, here it is — I hold it towards you."* — Keats, in the margin of an unfinished poem

## PHILOSOPHY

The site is not a book about Niels-Erik Nandal. It is his holograph — the single manuscript copy, in the author's own hand, still being written. Not a printed edition, not a scan of an antique: a living leaf, ruled and margined by the five-century canon, annotated in three inks, corrected in public, held toward the reader present-tense — *faciebat*, was making. This is art rather than decoration because nothing on the page is a picture of a manuscript; everything on the page is the *behavior* of one. The margins are derived, not padded. The red is a rubric, not a brand color. The crossings-out are real failures with real lessons. The apparatus of evidence — the site's moral signature — moves from a 13px afterthought to where scribes always put the proof: the margin, at the height of the claim, co-present with it, the way Rashi flanks the Talmud. A manuscript is the one book form that is simultaneously finished and alive. That is exactly what this man's site must be.

## THE EXPERIENCE

**Arrival.** No hero, no menu bar. A leaf. The text block sits visibly off-center — canon margins, 2:3:4:6 by ratio, the outer margin conspicuously the widest — and the first thing the eye meets is a rubricated incipit: a vermilion pilcrow, then *The Pillow Book of Niels-Erik Nandal* in Newsreader display italic at true display scale, the sublines beneath in text sizes. Above, in small iA Writer Quattro, a running head — site title left, section right — the way a book carries its own name at the top of every page. That running head *is* the navigation, on every leaf, forever. The feeling is unmistakable and physical: you have opened someone's book at its title leaf, and someone rules these pages by hand.

**The index.** Below the incipit, the table of contents: eight lists as eight numbered lines, oldstyle vermilion numerals, entry counts standing in the margin in celadon Quattro. One epigraph floats above it, keyed server-side to the actual season and hour — in spring, the dawn — because the Pillow Book was always time-indexed. No disclosure rows, no wall of collapsed content. A contents leaf promises leaves; it doesn't impersonate them.

**Opening a list.** A navigation, not an animation — the leaf simply turns. *Things that quicken the heart* opens with a vermilion drop cap (Newsreader, `initial-letter`, the body face at ceremony scale — never a costume font) and, as epigraph, its own finest line set alone in display italic with chirashigaki placement: "Sleep, with the diff still open," offset from the rule by a deliberate, repeatable measure. The corpus finally gets the scale it deserves. Then the entries: hanging em-dash paragraphs on a strict baseline grid, 66ch measure.

**Reading evidence.** This is the beat that changes everything. Beside an entry, in the wide outer margin, at the exact height of the claim, sits its proof — celadon Quattro, the annotator's ink: the link, the date, the commit, the source. No click. No `›`. Claim and evidence visibly co-present, main text and apparatus running in parallel like text and gloss on a Talmud page. On a phone, the gloss folds inline beneath its claim as a tap-open disclosure — it degrades to *nearer*, never to gone. The reader feels the site's ethic before they can name it: every sentence here stands next to its receipt.

**The log.** The ledger leaf. Dates hang in the margin, tabular Quattro; entries hold the column. A failure is struck through with a single sloped ink line — a real deletion, drawn once, not a CSS text-decoration — and its lesson stands in the margin in celadon: "A build that passes locally proves the laptop, not the deploy." The struck line stays fully legible under its stroke. Keats's crossed-out drafts survive because the crossing-out was part of the poem. Same here. The reader feels pathos in a ledger, which is the whole trick of a diary.

**The CV.** The fair copy — the one leaf written out clean for showing. Densest setting on the site, every line's citation as a marginal sidenote, and the print stylesheet derives its margins from the *same* canon ratios mapped to the physical sheet: the web leaf and the printed page are one manuscript. Reachable in one click from the running head of every page. The escape hatch is honest, fast, and the most beautiful résumé the recruiter has seen.

## ART DIRECTION

**The one signature move: the ruled margin.** Everything hangs on it. The text block is *constructed* — canon-derived, asymmetric, its outer margin reserved and wide — and that margin is where the site lives: evidence, glosses, dates, lessons, counts, running folio. Every other decision serves this one.

**Ink system — five tokens, four inks and a leaf:**
- **Leaf** — warm white ground (near `#FAF8F3`), a color, never a texture; no grain, no fibers, no filters, ever.
- **Ink** — near-black with the current `#16161D` blue-violet cast retained; the writing.
- **Shu** — vermilion (`≈#B5432E`, brightened slightly at night), the rubricator's seal-red: pilcrows, drop caps, folio numbers, section incipits, current-page mark in the running head. Structural jobs only, enumerable on one hand.
- **Celadon** — kept from the current site, promoted: the annotator's ink for all marginal apparatus, evidence, dates, tallies.
- **Murasaki** — kept, demoted and clarified: the binding thread. Links only. The purple that stitches leaf to leaf.

**Light.** The leaf is canonical. Dark mode is not an inversion but a scene: the desk at night — deep ink ground across the viewport, and the canon text block rendered as a faintly lighter field, the lamplit leaf lying on the dark desk. Chiaroscuro from two flat tones and geometry. No gradients anywhere in either theme.

**Type.** Newsreader stays and is finally used to its full range: optical sizes from caption to true display, italic display for incipits and epigraphs, oldstyle figures in prose. iA Writer Quattro stays as the working hand — margins, ledger, running heads, code — tabular figures. Two faces, no third, nothing tracked-out uppercase. Scale is a modular ladder derived from one baseline unit; every rule, heading, and gap lands on multiples of it.

**Space.** Ma is authored: a section break is exactly N baseline units of silence plus one vermilion mark, by rule. Chirashigaki appears in exactly two places — list epigraphs and the title leaf — with fixed, repeatable offsets. Asymmetry with a rule behind it is a hand; without one it's broken CSS.

**Motion.** None on wide viewports. Zero. The manuscript's condition is stillness; the only thing that moves is the reader. On narrow viewports, the single permitted motion is the 200ms unfold of a collapsed gloss. No sound.

## THE FACETS

- **Strongman** — the baseline grid is the bar path: one vertical rhythm, drawn down every page, nothing wobbling, zero layout shift ever; mass is expressed as type scale earned at incipits, not bold weight scattered around.
- **Hockey player** — the margin gloss is a saucer pass: claim released in the main column, proof landing flat in the margin exactly on the tape, aligned to the line of its reference — cross-column alignment as a completed pass.
- **Novelist** — the site keeps the full conventions of the codex without irony: running heads, folios, paragraph indents, chapter silence, a fair copy; prose is never interrupted by interface.
- **Poet** — single lines are granted whole ceremonies: epigraph leaves, display italic, scattered setting; the design's willingness to spend a page on one sentence *is* the poetics.
- **AI researcher** — the source is the second text: HTML written to be read, each leaf signed and colophoned in comments, llms.txt as the catalogue of the manuscript; the machine reads the same ruled book.
- **Engineer** — the canon is computed, not drawn: the margin ratios are a published formula in the colophon, the grid is inspectable, and the whole apparatus is CSS on semantic HTML that a linter can hold to account.

## SIGNATURE MOMENTS

1. A small colophon control (pure CSS, a checkbox) turns on **the ruling**: faint baseline lines and margin-construction geometry appear across the leaf — the manuscript showing its pricking and ruling, as every scribe's page was ruled before it was written.
2. Log failures are struck with a **single sloped SVG stroke in currentColor** — one rule, one slope, sitewide — never a distress effect, always legible beneath.
3. **View source** on any leaf finds a hand-set ASCII ruled header and the signature comment: *written in San Francisco; ruled by hand* — JODI's move in service of curl.
4. The **404 is an unwritten leaf**: pricking and ruling visible, margin empty, one rubric line — "This leaf was never written" — and the puck, kept, resting in the outer margin.
5. The **index epigraph knows the hour**: dawn in spring, night in winter — swapped server-side, invisible to JavaScript, faithful to Shōnagon's clock.
6. **Printing the CV** produces a sheet whose margins are the same canon at paper scale — hold it next to the screen and it is recognizably one book.

## MACHINE SURFACE

The plain HTML is the complete ground truth. Sidenotes live in DOM order directly after their claims (asides floated to the margin by CSS), so curl, reader mode, and crawlers get claim-then-evidence in perfect reading sequence — *better* structure than today's collapsed `<details>`. Strikes are semantic `<del>` with lessons as adjacent `<ins>`-style asides. Running heads are `<nav>` landmarks. The ruling toggle, chirashigaki offsets, canon margins: all pure CSS, invisible to machines, degrading to a clean linear document. JSON-LD, llms.txt, RSS, sitemap, byte-identical canonical bio: untouched. Evidence validation still gates the build. The register is byte-sacred; not one word of copy moves. What degrades: geometry. What is identical: every word, every link, every proof.

## SACRIFICES

- **The disclosure homepage dies.** Lists become real leaves, one click deeper. The contents page stops pretending to be the whole book — a manuscript is entered, not scanned.
- **Night yields the throne.** The leaf becomes canonical; dark becomes the desk scene. "Night writing" survives as the more atmospheric theme, but the manuscript's ground truth is the page.
- **The one animation is spent down to zero.** The ink-reveal goes. Stillness is the stronger claim.
- **Murasaki is demoted** from headings to thread. A quieter site in exchange for three inks that each mean exactly one thing.
- **Wide screens spend real estate on emptiness.** The canon's reserved margin will look "wasteful" to a dashboard eye. That waste is the ma; it is the point.
- **No Turner, no atmosphere, no generative surface.** This vision refuses the painted ground entirely. One metaphor, total.

## GRAVITY

**Hard to build:** the margin apparatus is the risk and the treasure — sidenote alignment at reference height, collision resolution when glosses stack, the inline collapse on narrow viewports, print parity. Budget the majority of engineering there; everything else is tokens and type. The struck-line SVG must be one reusable rule (fixed slope, one stroke-width, currentColor) or it drifts into grunge.

**Where kitsch lives, and the police:** (1) *Vermilion creep* — the moment shu decorates a hover or a background it is a brand color; the lint is an enumerated whitelist of rubric jobs, everything else fails review. (2) *Drop-cap costume* — body face only, two sizes only, section-opens only. (3) *Paper cosplay* — `background-image`, `filter`, and texture assets are banned in globals.css outright; the leaf is a hex value. (4) *Margin clutter* — Gwern's failure mode; hard cap of one gloss per entry, and the margin never contains anything that isn't apparatus. (5) *Canon literalism* — port the logic (derived, asymmetric, reserved), never the fixed-sheet geometry; below 60rem the margin folds and the book becomes a scroll, honestly.

**What could go wrong:** the site reads as antique instead of alive. The defense is already in the design: no textures, zero motion, computed margins, a time-aware epigraph, a ledger that changed yesterday, and crossings-out dated last week. Antiques are finished. This manuscript visibly is not — *faciebat*, present tense, held toward you.
