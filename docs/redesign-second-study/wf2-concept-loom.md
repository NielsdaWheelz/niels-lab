CANDIDATES

1. **Reverse Tapestry** — the site shows only the felted, knotted back of a tapestry by default, the structurally honest side, with a rare deliberate gesture to glimpse the finished front.
2. **Selvage** — the eight Shonagon lists become the selvage, the narrow reinforced edge-bands of a bolt of cloth that carry the weaver's mark, while the body of the page stays nearly bare.
3. **Boro** — the whole biography as a boro garment, decades of sashiko-stitched patches sewn over old tears, where the mending stitches, not the patches, are what you click to move through it.
4. **The Drawloom Program** — a working drawloom whose hidden punch-card pattern, the real lineage from drawloom to Jacquard card to Babbage to binary, both encodes the six lives and doubles as the site's literal machine-readable manifest.
5. **Warp-Weighted Loom** — an ancient loom held in shape only by real hanging weights, strongman plates standing in for the stone loom-weights of the Moirai, so the fabric visibly sags wherever a thread has gone slack.

## TEXERE

**Epigraph:** *Text, from texere, to weave. Everything below is cloth — read the underside first.*

**THE IDEA.** The site is a loom mid-work, rendered as a length of fabric you meet from the back. Six warp threads — hockey, strongman, novelist, poet, researcher, engineer — run fixed, vertical, unchanging, the whole height of the page. Time is the weft: one horizontal pick added per /log entry, per essay, per shipped thing. On arrival you see the reverse side — the honest, ungainly structure a weaver actually reads to know the cloth is right: dense semantic HTML, plain grid, no ornament, exactly the "curl-clean" surface the brief demands, because here that surface isn't a compromise, it's correct textile practice. Beside it hangs a punch-card strip, a column of filled and open circles — a real drawloom pattern card, and also the page's literal structured-data manifest. One control, *turn the cloth*, flips the same markup to its front: the identical data, restyled as the finished figured weave, Newsreader set into the visible face of the pattern. Nothing is added between the two views. Nothing is hidden either.

**HOW THE LISTS LIVE IN IT.** The eight Shonagon lists are the selvage — the reinforced edge-bands, one rail each side, that keep a woven width from unraveling and traditionally carry the mill's mark. Each entry is a cord. Click one and it doesn't navigate anywhere; it threads that line as a weft pick clear across the current panel, crossing all six warp threads at once, and every crossing where the pick actually catches a thread knots visibly — showing you, mechanically, which of the six selves that one line of prose actually touched.

**ONE SIGNATURE MOMENT.** The lever. You've been reading a dry, correct grid of dated entries; you pull *turn the cloth*; the same words, same order, nothing added, resolve into a portrait — the way a weaver on the shop floor actually flips a bolt to check the face. People will describe the lever, not the picture.

**WHY ART, NOT GIMMICK.** Text and textile share one Latin verb because writing already was weaving before anyone made it a metaphor. The site doesn't decorate a life with thread imagery; it returns a written record to what recording has always literally been. The back/front duality states the one true fact about any documented life: the ungainly substrate (dates, code, false starts, knots) and the composed image others eventually see are the same threads, not two accounts. It survives visit ten because a returning reader will keep to the back on purpose — the underside is for people who want the record still being made, not the finished portrait, which is also Montaigne's whole method.

**THE FACETS.**
- *Hockey:* new picks in /log snap into place with one hard, unequal-eased CSS stop — the blunt physical grammar of a puck killed dead by a stick — against the essays' slow float.
- *Strongman:* warp threads render taut and thick only where a self is currently active, slack and thin where dormant — the page's own weight registering exertion.
- *Novelist:* a long float — a thread skipping visibly, uncrossed, across years of unrelated picks — traces one novel's arc unbroken beneath everything else.
- *Poet:* that selvage rail alone keeps hand-beaten, irregular pick spacing against an otherwise machine-regular grid — the one visible meter on the page.
- *AI researcher:* the punch-card strip is the real JSON-LD manifest rendered as holes — SEO and agent-legibility made into the actual historical Jacquard card, inspectable, not implied.
- *Engineer:* the turn is one CSS custom-property flip, zero duplicated markup — view-source is the proof of elegance, not a claim of it.

**THE RISK.** Kitsch failure: craft-fair textile skeuomorphism — drawn shuttles, gingham, cross-stitch fonts. The rule: nothing woven is ever illustrated. Every thread, weight, and card must be the actual data structure rendered as itself — real grid, real transform, real manifest. If a weaving element needs a drawing to explain it, it is cut.
