i · Diagnosis

Correct is not the same as beautiful

The Pillow Book v1 succeeded at everything it policed and failed at the one thing it couldn’t lint. Total coherence — six tokens, two faces, one animation — and almost nothing sensuous. The survey put its finger on five wounds:

  1. The corpus is a poem the design sets like a memo. “Sleep, with the diff still open” gets the same 1.125rem treatment as footer furniture. Nothing is ever granted display scale, silence, or a page of its own.
  2. The moral signature is visually the most timid thing on the site. Claim-wired-to-evidence is the whole ethic, and it renders as a 13px ›.
  3. The palette never gets a moment. Murasaki and celadon exist only as small-size trim; “night writing” is a stated concept with no material presence.
  4. Every room wears the same shell. Homepage, log, CV, lab — one centered column, no rhythm shift, no architecture.
  5. The log’s pathos is invisible. Struck failures with attached lessons are a diary device with real weight, rendered as indistinguishable rows in a plain list.

The failure is not austerity. It is that the austerity was never once interrupted by magnificence.

ii · The Laws

What every lens converged on

Seven research digests, three judges, and one critic agree on more than they disagree. These are the laws any direction must obey — the constitution under whichever plate wins.

  1. One metaphor, fully committed, or none. The tell of kitsch is always incompletion — a garden that is just a blog, a car that drives nowhere. Bruno Simon and gwern sit at opposite poles of the same law.
  2. Words win the first half-second. If atmosphere takes the eye before the sentence does, contrast is miscalibrated. The room serves the sitter.
  3. Every unit of light, weight, or ornament traces to a real datum — or it is cut. A glow that means nothing is a gradient with an alibi.
  4. Two tempos: instant and geological. Nothing between. Anything cycling faster than minutes reads as screensaver; Eno’s test — noticed by staring, never by interruption.
  5. Choreograph the scroll; build one heavy room. The reader’s scroll is the one animation no designer can remove, and no vision used it. One surface at architectural scale, traveled through, the reader’s hand as the only easing curve — this is where the hockey player and the strongman live as felt experience instead of caption.
  6. The site must be able to smile. Shōnagon is arch, quick, catty; Austen is funny. Sixteen concepts built sixteen mausoleums for a man who is not dead. The deepest register of a life is reached faster through delight than through sediment.
  7. The lists are the anti-silo. Never a facet-nav. “Things that are exact: a wrist shot, a deadlift, a line of iambic pentameter” — the zuihitsu already dissolves taxonomy. Any design that re-siloes the six selves, then labors to reconnect them, has misread the genre. The lists, not the lives, are the load-bearing walls.
  8. Plain HTML is the ground truth; the source is the second text. curl, reader mode, and agents get the complete book. View-source finds the page more composed underneath, never less. Machine legibility gains facts, never loses them.
  9. Nothing is locked, only surfaced first. Whatever selective disclosure the design uses, the full text is always one honest move away. Earned revelation layers on top of legibility, never replaces it.
  10. The honesty rules compile. Every plate ends in a governing rule, and every governing rule is prose intention until it becomes a build-time lint: no disclosure without evidence, no computed weight without a traceable input, no mark in the source that isn’t true. The mound’s own trowel, sharpened into a test suite. This is the one law that keeps all the others alive under deadline.
  11. No costume. The traditions are sources for structure and rhythm, never surface: no parchment texture, no cork board, no pixel that depicts stone. Period lives in diction and apparatus or not at all.

iii · The Plates

Four directions, each fully committed

Distilled from three full visions and fifteen concepts, adversarially demolished, judged through three lenses. Each plate is buildable alone. The verdicts are quoted without mercy.

PLATE I

The Living Hand

“This living hand, now warm and capable — see, here it is — I hold it towards you.” — Keats

The site as holograph: the single manuscript copy, in the author’s own hand, still being written. Canon-derived asymmetric margins (2:3:4:6, computed, published in the colophon) with the wide outer margin as the site’s reserved zone — and that margin is where the ethic becomes architecture: evidence stands beside its claim, at the height of the claim, celadon apparatus running parallel to ink text the way Rashi flanks the Talmud. No click, no ›. Four inks and a leaf: ink, vermilion rubric (structural jobs only, enumerable on one hand), celadon apparatus, murasaki thread (links only). The leaf becomes canonical; dark becomes a scene — the lamplit page on the night desk. Zero motion on wide viewports. The manuscript’s condition is stillness; the only thing that moves is the reader.

Signature momentIn the log, a failure is struck with a single sloped stroke — one rule, one slope, sitewide — and its lesson stands in the margin: “A build that passes locally proves the laptop, not the deploy.” Keats’s crossed-out drafts survive because the crossing-out was part of the poem.

Risk: medieval cosplay by accumulation — pilcrow + drop cap + incipit + folio is six antiquarian signifiers stacking into a Renaissance faire of restraint. And the deeper charge sticks: this cures “correct but not beautiful” with more rigorous correctness. It ships only if it takes the monument and the smile from the laws above.

PLATE II

Bearing

Everything below bears everything above.

The site as load-bearing wall. Every element has mass — measured, not asserted: at build time a published pure function assigns each line its setting from real quantities, weight(el) = f(depth, bytes, evidence, age), rendered on the variable weight axis of the faces the site already owns. A page opens light and settles, course by course, toward a stout foundation. The semantic inversion is the whole ethic: assertion is light, evidence is dense — because evidence is what actually holds the claim up. Poems are exempt: verse alone hangs from above, unloaded — the one typographic law that is itself a poem. /now is ice: the lightest page on the site, present tense gliding, nothing settled yet. The footer counts plates onto the bar: this page weighs 38,214 bytes and bears seven years.

Signature momentThe /log: seven years in one column, and the gradient runs across years. What a visitor tells a friend: “His past is literally denser. The old entries are load-bearing.”

walk through it — feel the wall settle →

Risk: physics toy — scroll-triggered boldening, illegible slabs, strain performed instead of borne. The police: no element may display a weight it was not computed to carry, and the whole gradient lives inside legibility. If you notice the mechanism before you feel it, the setting is wrong.

PLATE III

The Book of Hours

In spring, the dawn. The sky does the moving so the words can keep still.

Turner on the web: every page is the same page at a different hour. One painted ground — thirty stacked near-transparent washes, seeded deterministically by date, hour, season — beneath austere typography held in a clearing: a stabilized pocket whose contrast is clamped to AA whatever the sky is doing. Light is wired to fact: the reader’s real hour, the season, the recency of the log. Cold dawn is rink light at 6 a.m., kept honest and unnamed. Two silent weeks and the ledger clouds over — the site’s conscience, visible from the doorway. The hour dial subsumes the theme toggle. One step into the CV and the sky evaporates entirely: ink on white, a records office — proof the weather elsewhere was chosen.

Signature momentAt 4:47 a.m. the site stands at its deepest ink and Things that quicken the heart is the list standing open — Shōnagon’s clock choosing the door. The design’s whole thesis in one dawn visit.

walk through it — turn the hour dial, watch the door change →

Risk: disqualifying at partial budget. Thirty tuned washes × six states × seasonal tilt is weeks of taste-work, and the critic’s finding is exact: amputated to presets, a murasaki-bruised dark gradient is literally the banned purple gradient. This plate ships at 100% or not at all. Second finding: the conscience mechanism has no mercy rule — a recruiter arriving mid-vacation sees a site performing depression.

PLATE IV

Tell

Every stratum was once the surface.

The site as excavation report of an inhabited mound. “A man who has been five things is not five men; he is one mound” — the truest sentence anyone wrote in either fleet. A typed section drawing is the navigation: the occupation layer alive at the top (/now, /log), numbered strata descending — research, engineering, the novels, the poems, the iron — and the profile bottoms out at ice, not soil. The lists are registers of small finds, each entry catalogued, cross-referenced (cf. NN-071); following the threads is the entire game, and the only save file is the visitor’s mind. The site accessions its own log: entries older than a season are formally compacted into the stratum below. The living site buries itself.

Signature momentThe ice stratum is bedrock: everything above cross-references down into it; it references nothing below, because there is nothing below. Hockey given the load-bearing position every literary design denies it.

walk through it — read the wall, open the finds →

Risk: apparatus that catalogues feeling instead of producing it — the beauty judge scored the mound a 6 and preferred the grief of its own discarded candidate. And theme-park archaeology waits one texture away. The police: nothing enters that could not appear in a real excavation monograph.

iv · Recommendation

Plate V — The Holograph

A holograph: a work wholly in the author’s own hand. The manuscript, given mass.

walk through it — the book, arriving directly →

page and wall, side by side — the same corpus set twice, and the five choices unbundled →

The plates above are pure commitments; this is the composition I would build. It is not a merge of everything — the carnival is the one guaranteed failure — but one governing metaphor carrying one physics, one clock, one monument, under a strict graft budget. The metaphor is Plate I’s: the living manuscript. The physics is Plate II’s: weight, computed from truth. They are not two ideas. A manuscript is a physical object; ink laid on over years has mass; the page settles. Bearing is what The Living Hand feels like when it stops being weightless.

Architecture
The Living Hand entire: computed canon margins, the reserved outer margin as the site’s home for apparatus — evidence beside claim, at claim height. Four inks and a leaf. Leaf canonical; night as the desk scene.
Physics
Bearing’s weight function as the site’s only typographic mechanism, subliminal, published in the colophon as a model card. Assertion light, evidence dense. Poems hang.
Clock
Shōnagon’s hour, server-side: the index epigraph and which list stands open follow the real San Francisco hour and season. No JavaScript; without a clock the site is canonically the small hours.
Monument
Cut, 2026-09-05, client’s hand. The scroll threshold is struck. The critic’s charge — nobody built anything heavy — must now be answered by the body itself: the settling type carries the mass, or nothing does.
Ledger
Ink-age confined to /log, where recency is meaning — and asymmetric: a struck failure fades faster than the lesson beside it. After a year, what remains dark is what was learned.
Stone
The CV is the one page outside the night and outside time: daylight canonical, dense, print-perfect, margins derived from the same canon at paper scale. What is carved does not age.
Source
Foul papers: the rendered page is the fair copy, the HTML source the working draft — comments holding true struck lines, reverted SHAs, missed lifts. Every mark real or absent. The colophon comment signs each leaf: written in San Francisco; ruled by hand.
Smile
A wit budget, spent deadpan: the 404’s lineage extended to one dry aside per room — the running head, the empty states, the colophon — Shōnagon’s temper, never a wink. Delight is a requirement, not a garnish.
Lint
Law 10 as CI: evidence validation extended to the weight function’s inputs, the hour tables, the source apparatus. A mark that cannot prove itself fails the build.

Refused, by name: the painted skies (Plate III stands or falls whole — it does not donate parts); strata numbering and find-tags outside the log; ink-age anywhere but the ledger; drop caps and rubricated folios together (pick the rubric, drop the costume); any second display-scale room; X-ray, theater, and loom imagery entire; the contradicting field note — the single best anti-slop device in the fleet, but it is new prose, which is your pen, not mine. It goes to the content phase as a question, not a graft.

vi · Questions

Answers that change the spec

  1. Which plate? I–IV pure, or V as composed. Everything downstream — spec, schemas, workstreams — waits on this one choice.
  2. May the leaf take the throne? Plates I and V make light canonical and demote “night writing” to the desk scene. Plate III keeps night sovereign. This is a real philosophical reversal of v1, not a token swap.
  3. Does the raw material exist? Foul papers and the mutating sentence run on real struck drafts, real missed lifts, real reverted SHAs, nineteen real years of one first line. If the archive doesn’t exist, those devices are cut — nothing is ever fabricated into an evidence-sacred site.
  4. The field notes: the contradicting specimen label needs new prose in your hand, one label per entry, each a shade less flattering than its claim. Content phase, question-skeletons only — do you want the skeletons drafted?

On your go with a plate chosen: the lean spec — schemas, non-goals, acceptance criteria, non-overlapping workstreams — then implementation on a second go, hard cutover, no legacy paths, branch only.
