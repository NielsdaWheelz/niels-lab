CANDIDATES

1. **Tidal Ledger** — the record surfaces and submerges with San Francisco's actual tide height right now, so roughly half of it is always literally underwater and unreadable.
2. **Horarium** — the eight monastic canonical hours, computed from real solar position rather than clock time, each hour opening exactly one of the eight lists and closing the rest.
3. **Phenological Field** — a farmer's-almanac planting chart keyed to SF's real fog and dry seasons, where each list germinates, leafs, flowers, and goes to seed on its own calendar.
4. **Dead Reckoning** — a ship's log with no fixed homepage, only a position fixed fresh each visit from today's real sun and moon altitude, the lists logged as ports of call.
5. **Lectionary** — one fragment of the record licensed per actual calendar date across a 365-day cycle, so the whole life can only be read once, slowly, over a year of returning.

# HORARIUM

*Not every hour wants to be a strongman.*

**THE IDEA.** The site is a working horarium — the schedule of the eight canonical hours (Matins, Lauds, Prime, Terce, Sext, None, Vespers, Compline), computed live from San Francisco's actual sun, not from clock time. Arrive, and the first thing you see is a single line — *Now: Sext, the sun stands over the yard* — over a header whose color is the real current sky, derived from solar elevation. Below it, one list stands open: the hour's list. The other seven sit shut, named but closed, in a column split into *hours not yet come* and *hours already kept*. Nothing is locked — a recruiter at 2am can still open every list — but what greets you, unbidden, is only ever the truth of that hour. A thin timeline bar along the bottom, marked with today's actual sunrise, moonrise, and tide, shows where "now" sits.

**HOW THE LISTS LIVE IN IT.** Each of the eight lists is married to the hour whose register it shares — the one nearest the body goes to first light, the one nearest sleep to Compline — so the skeleton doesn't decorate the form, it staffs it. A visitor doesn't browse eight lists; they meet whichever one is on duty.

**ONE SIGNATURE MOMENT.** Stay on the page as a real hour boundary crosses — say Sext tips into None — and watch it happen without a refresh: the sky-color header drifts over twenty seconds, the open list's border draws closed, and the next one unfurls beside it while the header line updates itself in place. You are not told time has passed. You watch the sundial's shadow actually move.

**WHY ART, NOT GIMMICK.** Most portfolio sites flatten a person into simultaneity — every achievement co-present, as if all selves are always on duty. A horarium refuses that lie: it insists identity is metered, not stacked — that the strongman is not awake at Matins, that the poet does not answer at noon. This is truer to a life than a résumé is, and it is why the site outlives visit ten: arriving at dawn, at midnight, in fog season or in October's hard clear light gives a genuinely different page, not a randomized one — tied to the same sky standing over the same city the reader is standing under. The only way to see all of it is what it takes to know a person: time, and returning.

**THE FACETS.**
- *Hockey player* — Prime's timeline segment is drawn as three period clocks, not a smooth bar.
- *Strongman* — Sext carries the heaviest type weight and widest column on the page: the loaded hour, typographically.
- *Novelist* — Vespers is the one hour where the list unfurls as running prose instead of bulleted lines.
- *Poet* — Matins renders in the thinnest, smallest type on a near-black field: the hour almost no one arrives for.
- *AI researcher* — None is headed by a live build timestamp and git SHA instead of prose: the machine hour looks machine-made.
- *Engineer* — Compline is where the site's own deploy log lives, rendered as the day's final office: the day, put to bed.

**THE RISK.** The failure mode is costume — illuminated capitals, candle glyphs, faux-monkish copy, a Ren-faire almanac. The governing rule: no hour may be announced by anything but real astronomical arithmetic and plain type. If a design choice cannot be justified by an actual number — solar elevation, minutes to sunset, tide in feet — it is cut. The horarium is allowed to tell time. It is never allowed to tell a story about telling time.
