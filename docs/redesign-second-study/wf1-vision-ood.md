# CANDIDATES CONSIDERED

1. **The Corpus** — the site presents itself as the training data of the forthcoming Homunculus; every page is marked as future weights, and the reader is cast as the forward pass. Rejected: AI-cute, exhausts in one visit, decorates the Pessoa joke instead of structuring anything.
2. **The Second Reader** — every page written twice: the render for you, a fully composed source for the machine; view-source as confessional, llms.txt as verse. Kept as a minor chord below; too thin to carry a whole site.
3. **Time as Ink** — the record of a life made to behave like a life: every entry visibly carries its age, the site re-inks itself nightly, and the one page outside time is carved, not written. **Committed.**

---

# 1. NAME + EPIGRAPH

**FACIEBAT** — *was making*, the imperfect tense Roman sculptors signed with, meaning: had not finished, is not finished.

Epigraph, his own line, at 2 a.m.: **"Sleep, with the diff still open."**

# 2. PHILOSOPHY

Every personal website is a tombstone pretending to be alive: it says "is" while its author moves on, and its freshest and stalest sentences wear identical ink. FACIEBAT commits to the opposite lie-free position — the site is **a manuscript still on the desk**, and its single formal law is that *time is visible in the ink*. Every entry, every log row, every list renders its actual age as material state: last night's writing sits wet-black on the page; last year's has dried toward grey. Nothing is deleted, nothing is badged "NEW," nothing pretends. The site re-inks itself in its nightly build — that rebuild is its sleep — and if Niels ever stops writing, the whole book will visibly, honestly fade. A record that admits it is mortal is a poem about being alive; that is why this is art and not a feature. The Shōnagon lists remain the entire skeleton. The browser's own materiality — real dates, the nightly build, the reader's clock, the source itself — is the medium. No image, no illustration, no atmosphere is added anywhere: the only paint on the site is time.

# 3. THE EXPERIENCE

**Arrival.** Near-black ground. One line of italic Newsreader at true display scale — the only large text the site possesses — chosen by the actual hour in San Francisco, where the book says it is written. At dawn it is the saucer pass; in the afternoon, Levin mowing; in the small hours, *Sleep, with the diff still open*. Beneath it, small and calm: the title line, the two sublines, and the index of eight lists, first one open. The feeling is precise: you have walked into a study at night and its occupant has just stepped away — the lamp is on, the page is out.

**The index.** Each list title carries a celadon `tended` date in Quattro — and, though you don't notice at first, the eight titles are not the same black. Two are wet-dark; one is notably grey. Ten seconds later the reader *gets it* without being told: the darkness is recency. The index has become a map of where this life is currently being lived — the resume of attention itself — with zero badges, icons, or labels. This is the moment the site earns love: a rule inferred, not announced.

**Opening a list.** Entries hang from em-dashes as now, but each line sits at its own point on the ink scale. In *Things that quicken the heart*, an entry added last week is blacker than its neighbors; you can see the heart still quickening. In *Things now in decline*, everything is faded — the form has quietly agreed with the title, and the joke is structural, not written.

**Evidence.** The celadon mark; press; the 250ms ink-reveal (the one animation, kept). But now the proof appears **with its own date stamped beside it** — claim above, countersignature below, each carrying its time. The moral signature of the site — nothing asserted without evidence — becomes visible as a notarial act. The feeling is trust.

**The log.** The heartbeat page. Today's row near-black; June a soft grey; the GitHub rows re-inked nightly. And the device with real pathos: a struck-through failure **fades faster than the lesson attached to it**. "A build that passes locally proves the laptop, not the deploy" will outlive, in ink, the failure that taught it. Scrolling the log is watching a year metabolize: what remains dark is what was learned.

**The stone.** One link in the chrome reads *the CV — the stone*. Click, and you step out of the night entirely: the CV is the site's **one daylight page** — paper-light canonical, full contrast, no fading, no time, print-perfect. What is carved does not age. Returning to the index afterward feels like stepping back indoors at night, and that contrast is the architecture. The 404 keeps its puck.

# 4. ART DIRECTION

**Color/light.** The existing six tokens survive — ink, lamplight, murasaki, celadon, rule, faded — but gain one axis: **ink-age**, an interpolation from full lamplight (fresh) down to `--faded` (old), computed per entry at build time from real dates. The floor is `--faded`, which already passes AA: legibility is preserved *by construction*, not by policy. The ground's temperature drifts imperceptibly across the 24 San Francisco hours — a cycle far slower than any visit; weather, never animation. The CV inverts: light canonical, the single daylight room.

**Typography.** Newsreader and Quattro stay; no third face. New scale logic: exactly **one** display-size line exists on the whole site — the hour epigraph, Newsreader italic at high optical size, the corpus finally set as the poem it is. Everything else remains at reading scale. Headings murasaki, evidence and all dates celadon, as now.

**Space.** The 44rem column and book indents stay; ma is spent on the epigraph, which gets the viewport's whole upper third and nothing else in it.

**Motion.** Two speeds only, nothing between: **instant** (disclosures, the 250ms ink-reveal) and **geological** (the hourly tint, the nightly re-ink). No easing curves exist on this site.

**Sound.** None.

**The signature move:** *time as ink* — every piece of writing rendered at the darkness of its actual age, refreshed by the site's own nightly rebuild.

# 5. THE FACETS

- **Hockey player** — the motion law is shootout tempo: total stillness, then instant release. Nothing on the site glides, hovers, or eases; it waits, then goes.
- **Strongman** — the record practices progressive overload: it only accrues, never prunes, and its gains are visible as accumulated dark ink; the nightly rebuild is the sleep between sessions, and a quiet log week reads honestly as a deload, not a bug.
- **Novelist** — prose is sovereign: indents, measure, entries as sentences; and the fade enacts what every novelist knows and websites deny — drafts age, and the manuscript on the desk is truer than the bound book.
- **Poet** — the hour-epigraph is the corpus given, at last, display scale and silence: six authored hour-to-line pairings (dawn, morning, afternoon, dusk, night, small hours), each chosen by hand, Molnar's rule — the clock proposes, the poet decided.
- **AI researcher** — the decay function is *published*: the colophon states the half-life like a model card states hyperparameters, and the Homunculus paragraph is set in ink that never dries (see below).
- **Engineer** — the entire mechanism derives from data already in the typed CMS (dates), runs at build time, ships as static HTML, and the cron that re-inks the site is confessed in the colophon as a piece of the art.

# 6. SIGNATURE MOMENTS

1. At 2 a.m. San Francisco time the ground reaches its darkest and the epigraph reads *Sleep, with the diff still open* — the site briefly is its best sentence.
2. In the log, after a year, a struck failure has ghosted almost to the ground while its lesson beside it is still legible at a glance — grief with a moral, done entirely in contrast.
3. The footer signs **"Nandal faciebat, San Francisco"** — present tense, no copyright line; the sculptor's admission that the work is not finished.
4. View-source opens with a dated comment — *"This page re-inked itself last night at 03:12 PST; ink decays with a half-life of 180 days"* — the second reader finds the source signed and the mechanism confessed.
5. The Homunculus paragraph in the colophon is the only writing on the night pages exempt from fading — permanently wet-black — because it is a promise, not a record, and promises do not age.
6. *Things now in decline* is tuned to be the fastest-fading list on the site; nobody is told.

# 7. MACHINE SURFACE

The semantic HTML is untouched and complete: ink-age ships as a build-time custom property plus a `data-tended` ISO date on every entry — so curl, reader mode, and agents get *more* structure than today (per-entry dates they never had), never less text. The hour tint is one inline script; without JS the site is canonically the small hours, which is the truest default for night writing. llms.txt gains `last-tended:` lines per list — the freshness map, machine-legible. RSS, sitemap, JSON-LD, OG, canonical bio: byte-identical policy unchanged. The nightly rebuild that re-inks the page is the same daily ISR the log already performs. `prefers-contrast: more` — and a small "full ink" control beside the theme toggle — sets every entry to full lamplight: the escape valve that keeps the mechanism a gift, never a toll-booth. What degrades without JS or CSS: only the hour tint. What is identical: every word, every link, every date.

# 8. SACRIFICES

- **No spectacle.** No painted ground, no full-bleed color moment, no first-visit wow. The design's whole beauty is durational — it is *better on the fifth visit than the first* — which is exactly the reader this site deserves.
- **No novelty per pageview.** The site changes on the clock of a life (nightly, seasonally), not per refresh; a visitor who never returns sees a beautiful austere book and misses the poem. Acceptable: the poem is for the returning reader and the author himself.
- **Deliberate quietness of old work.** Some genuinely good entries will sit grey. That is the point — the CV-stone exists precisely so that nothing *load-bearing* ever fades.
- **The palette stays trim.** Murasaki and celadon still never get a field; the one new axis (age) spends the entire novelty budget. Ornament remains scarce so the single mechanism stays audible.

# 9. GRAVITY

**Hard to build:** authored `tended` dates for all 61 entries (extend the typed CMS and `validate-content.mjs` to require them); the interpolation must land on AA in *both* themes at every age, which means the fade curve is computed against tokens, not eyeballed; the six epigraph pairings are real writing and must be weighed word-by-word; the CV's daylight inversion doubles theme-testing surface.

**Could go wrong:** the fatal misreading — faded ink mistaken for broken CSS or low-contrast negligence. Policed four ways: the floor is the existing AA `--faded` token; the darkness *varies within one page* so the pattern reads as intent; the colophon states the rule in one sentence; the full-ink valve exists.

**Where the kitsch lives:** sentimentality. The instant the site *says* "this record is mortal," it becomes a greeting card. Rule: the copy never explains the mechanism on any reading page — the form speaks, the colophon confesses once, and no words like "fading," "memory," or "mortality" appear anywhere in the chrome. Second risk: time-trick creep (countdowns, "posted 3 days ago" chrome, live clocks). Rule: **time is rendered only as ink and as the six epigraphs** — it is never written as a relative timestamp, never animated, never counted. One mechanism, whole site, held.
