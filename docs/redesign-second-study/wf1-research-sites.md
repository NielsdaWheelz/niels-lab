# The Pantheon: Mechanisms Behind Extraordinary Personal Websites

## Exemplars

**Bret Victor — worrydream.com** — [worrydream.com](https://worrydream.com/). The navigation is not a menu, it's an argument: dozens of page icons sit at hand-set angles across the screen; clicking one sends every other icon flying off-screen while the chosen piece slides into focus, and browser scroll conventions (Page Down, arrow keys) simply don't work. The mechanism is total commitment to a spatial, gestural metaphor — the site behaves like the ideas inside it (non-linear, manipulable, resistant to passive consumption) rather than describing them.

**Gwern — gwern.net** — [gwern.net/design](https://gwern.net/design). "Semantic zoom": every page readable at seven depths (title → abstract → margin note → sidenote → body → collapsed aside → hover-popup), so a skimmer and an obsessive get different but equally complete experiences from the same page. The rule that makes it art rather than clutter: "you must earn your ornaments" — dropcaps, link icons, and backlinks exist only because they carry information, never as decoration.

**Bruno Simon — bruno-simon.com** — [bruno-simon.com](https://bruno-simon.com/). You drive a toy car through a 3D world; his resume, projects, and contact form are buildings you crash into. The mechanism is a metaphor followed to its logical end rather than gestured at — this is the difference between "playful" as a word in a case study and playfulness as the entire architecture.

**Lynn Fisher — lynnandtonic.com** — [lynnandtonic.com](https://lynnandtonic.com/). Widening the browser window causes illustrated cracks on the page to split open and reveal more content beneath — turning a purely technical constraint (responsive breakpoints) into a narrative gesture. She rebuilds the whole site from scratch every year as a public ritual of craft, so the site's history becomes part of the work.

**Craig Mod — craigmod.com** — [craigmod.com](https://craigmod.com/). Essays are paced like a walk: full-bleed photographs breaking long paragraphs at a deliberate cadence down an endless vertical scroll, with no sidebar, no related-posts rail, nothing competing with the rhythm of image-then-text-then-image. The mechanism is structure-as-tempo — the scroll itself is edited, the way a film is edited.

**Robin Sloan — robinsloan.com** — [robinsloan.com](https://www.robinsloan.com/). A flat, unhierarchical index of wildly disparate work — novels, an olive oil company, a band, software experiments — presented with equal plainness and no attempt to spin it into a single "personal brand." The mechanism is refusal to flatten a many-sided life into one marketable identity; the honesty of the list is the design.

**Maggie Appleton — maggieappleton.com/garden** — [maggieappleton.com/garden](https://maggieappleton.com/garden/). Content is explicitly sorted by epistemic status — seedling notes, growing essays, evergreen library entries — so the site's structure literally models how a mind holds ideas at different stages of confidence. The mechanism: information architecture as intellectual honesty, not just navigation.

**Rauno Freiberg — rauno.me (2022 edition)** — [2022.rauno.me](https://2022.rauno.me/). The homepage is a desktop with a dock, ambient interface sounds, and windows you open — an operating-system metaphor for someone whose actual subject is interface craft. The mechanism: the interaction design is the portfolio; there is no separation between "here is my work" and "here is a demonstration of taste," because the site itself is the demonstration.

**Paco Coursey — paco.me** — [paco.me](https://paco.me/). Strict monochrome, one typeface, hierarchy built entirely from weight, size, and spacing — nothing else is permitted to carry meaning. The mechanism is subtraction as confidence: removing every device (color, icon, card) most sites lean on to prove you didn't need them.

**Tom MacWright — macwright.com** — [macwright.com](https://macwright.com/). The site has barely changed in years by explicit choice — he's written about resisting the urge to chase "the latest blog software or design." The mechanism is stability as a stance: refusing the redesign treadmill reads, paradoxically, as more considered than constant novelty.

**Frank Chimero — frankchimero.com** — [frankchimero.com](https://frankchimero.com/). Long-form essays set with oversized pull-quote typography, hand-drawn diagrams, and generous margins that make reading feel like sitting with a well-made book rather than scrolling a blog. The mechanism: type scale used as emotional punctuation, matching the register of the sentence to its size on the page.

**Cabel Sasser / Panic — cabel.com** — [cabel.com](https://cabel.com/). Plainspoken, specific, funny, unguarded writing about theme parks, old software, and the Playdate — zero corporate hedging, zero "passionate about." The mechanism: voice alone, with almost no visual invention, outperforms most heavily "designed" sites because a real person is legibly present in every sentence.

**HTML Energy / the handmade-web movement** — [kayserifserif.place](https://kayserifserif.place/posts/2025/html-day-and-celebrating-websites/). Not one site but an ethos: people writing raw HTML by hand, embracing "lopsided things," treating a personal page as a home or a studio rather than a funnel. The mechanism is visible seams — asymmetry, idiosyncratic nav, unresolved edges — functioning as proof of authorship, the opposite of frictionless template polish.

## Principles

1. **Commit one metaphor completely, or don't use one.** A half-followed device (a "garden" that's just a blog, a car you can't meaningfully drive anywhere) reads as kitsch; a metaphor followed to its structural end (Bruno Simon, gwern's semantic zoom) reads as art.
2. **Let structure encode meaning**, not just organize content — the information architecture should model how the person actually thinks (Appleton's epistemic tiers, Sloan's flat honest index).
3. **Typography and whitespace are usually the whole instrument** — before reaching for color, icons, or cards, ask what weight, size, and space alone can carry (paco.me, gwern, Chimero).
4. **Restraint and refusal to chase trends is a design decision**, not an absence of one (MacWright's non-redesign, Fisher's controlled palette inside wild concepts).
5. **Interaction should be the content**, not a delivery wrapper around it — the click/hover/scroll must be where the idea lives (Rauno's OS, worrydream's flight-navigation).
6. **Earn every ornament.** Decoration is permitted only when it does interpretive or functional work; cut what doesn't (gwern's explicit rule).
7. **Voice carries as much aesthetic weight as visuals.** Specific, unhedged, first-person writing (Sasser, Sloan) can make a visually plain site feel more alive than an elaborately "designed" one with generic copy.
8. **Design for the returning, attentive reader**, not the 5-second scanner — features that reward depth (popups, backlinks, annual rebuilds as ritual) rather than pure above-the-fold conversion optimization.

## Traps (2026 slop tells)

1. **Purple/indigo-to-cyan gradient hero with a glassmorphism card** — the single most recognizable AI-default fingerprint; instant "no human decided this."
2. **Three feature cards in a row** (icon, heading, two lines), regardless of subject — a value-prop grid imported from SaaS marketing, meaningless on a personal site.
3. **Decorative motion with no referent** — bounce/elastic easing, hover-lift on everything, pulsing status dots — kinetic garnish standing in for an actual interaction idea.
4. **One default sans (Inter/Geist) doing all hierarchy via size alone**, no typographic point of view, no paired display face, no earned scale.
5. **A borrowed metaphor that decorates instead of structures** — a device gestured at in copy ("welcome to my garden 🌱") but not built into the architecture; the tell of kitsch is always incompletion.
