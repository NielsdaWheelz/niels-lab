# DEMOLITION REPORT

## VISION A: THE MANUSCRIPT

**Derivative.** Strip the vermilion and this is Tufte-CSS plus Gwern's sidenotes plus the Van de Graaf canon — the three most-blogged moves in typography-nerd webdesign of the last decade. "Canon margins, marginalia at claim height, two faces, oldstyle figures" is a recognizable *genre*: the Butterick-adjacent literary-programmer site. The rubrication and drop caps are the only elements a reader couldn't find on practicaltypography.com, and drop caps are themselves a Medium-era cliché one costume-change away from kitsch.

**Kitsch risk and defense.** The defense is the best of the three — enumerated rubric jobs, texture ban, computed geometry. Credible. But it polices the wrong border. The danger isn't vermilion creep; it's *medieval cosplay by accumulation*: pilcrow + drop cap + incipit + folio + colophon + "faciebat" is six antiquarian signifiers on one site. Each defensible alone; together they read as a Renaissance-faire of restraint. The vision senses this ("antique instead of alive") and answers with *dated content* — but content freshness can't rescue form that smells of vitrine.

**Beautiful or correct?** Here is the fatal charge: the client's diagnosed failure is "austere and correct but NOT beautiful," and Vision A's cure is *more rigorous austerity with better margins*. Zero motion, no atmosphere, no color field, emptiness-as-virtue — this is the current site's temperament with a superior grid. Visit ten: excellent. Visit one: a beautifully set page, and the client already has a correctly set page. It refuses Turner, Rodin, and Cézanne *explicitly* — three of seven named touchstones sacrificed to "one metaphor, total." That's not commitment; that's amputating the half of the client that was asking for sensuousness.

**Facets.** "The margin gloss is a saucer pass" is rationalization, not design — no reader, ever, will perceive hockey in cross-column alignment. Strongman-as-baseline-grid likewise. The athlete facets are captioned onto engineering decisions that would exist anyway. Collapse into "literary engineer": complete.

**Gravity.** Honest about the risk: the margin apparatus is 70% of the value and 90% of the difficulty. Under effort pressure, sidenote collision-handling degrades to inline disclosures — *which is the current site*. Amputated Vision A is a font-size increase. It does not survive.

## VISION B: THE ATMOSPHERE

**Derivative.** Time-of-day-reactive personal sites are an established indie-web genre; "generative watercolor SVG washes" is Hobbs-imitation CodePen culture circa 2021; and "site reflects my commit activity" is the GitHub contribution graph wearing a Turner costume. A design-literate visitor's first association will be "lofi ambient study aesthetic" — the exact sensibility slop farms now mass-produce. This is the vision most likely to be mistaken, at a glance, for the thing Niels hates.

**Kitsch risk.** Highest of the three, and the defense — "every unit of light traces to a real datum" — is clever but *epistemically invisible*. The reader cannot verify that a glow is wired to a commit timestamp; they see a glow. A gradient with a published alibi is still, on the retina, a gradient. The celadon "pool of lamplight" behind evidence is one tasteful hand away from a hover-glow, and the vision's own police force is a standing order ("nothing glows without reason") that only the author can audit. Worse: the amputation scenario is disqualifying. Thirty tuned washes × six states × seasonal tilt is *weeks of taste-work*, admitted. Cut that under effort and the skies become presets — and a preset murasaki-bruised dark gradient **is literally the banned purple gradient**. Amputated Vision B is the forbidden object itself.

**Beautiful or clever?** Genuinely beautiful *if fully executed* — the only vision that swings at magnificence, and the CV-as-records-office contrast is a real aesthetic argument. The log-that-clouds-over is the most emotionally serious idea in any vision. But it also punishes the author: a recruiter arriving during a two-week vacation sees a site performing depression. The conscience mechanism has no mercy rule.

**Facets.** Cold-dawn-as-rink-light is the single best facet translation in all three visions — perceivable, unnamed, true. Strongman-as-tonnage-chiaroscuro is thin. Novelist and poet fare fine. Better than A, still tilted literary.

**Gravity.** Contrast across a computed continuum, tested per state, on a one-person art project — the AA invariant will be the first thing quietly broken. This vision survives only at full budget, and full budget is unlikely.

## VISION C: OUT OF DISTRIBUTION (FACIEBAT)

**Derivative.** This is the *digital garden* — Maggie Appleton's growth-stages, Gwern's epistemic-status tags, "evergreen notes" culture — with badges swapped for opacity. The swap is more elegant than the genre, but the genre is instantly recognizable to exactly the audience (AI researchers, engineers) this site courts. "My site shows which notes I've tended recently" is a known move.

**Kitsch/gimmick risk.** The stated defenses (AA floor, in-page variance, colophon, full-ink valve) address the *legibility* misreading but not the deeper one: gray text on dark ground is the signature of low-contrast negligence, the most-complained-about slop pattern on the modern web. The claim that readers "get it in ten seconds" is the vision's load-bearing optimism, unevidenced. But the fatal flaw is philosophical, not visual: **the mechanism conflates recency with vitality, and for a Pillow Book that is a category error.** Shōnagon's entries are a thousand years old and wet-black alive; a perfect entry about winter mornings does not stale. The design punishes finishedness — the poet's best, most settled lines fade *because they needed no revision* — and it quietly pressures Niels to perform "tending" theater to keep ink dark, corrupting the byte-sacred corpus with incentive. "Some genuinely good entries will sit grey. That is the point" — no. That is the bug, stated proudly.

**Beautiful?** It openly concedes no first-visit beauty. The brief demands "inspiring, magnificent"; visit one delivers the current austere site plus one large epigraph. The poem is real — lesson outliving failure is the finest single device in any vision — but it is a poem for an audience of one, and the client asked for a work of art, not a private mortality practice.

**Facets.** Shootout-tempo motion law: genuinely good, perceivable. Strongman-as-accrual: decent. Hockey: vestigial. Literary-engineer collapse: substantial.

**Gravity.** Lightest build; survives amputation best — one custom property per entry. But 61 retroactive `tended` dates are either real (from git, sparse) or authored (fabricated), and fabricated dates on an evidence-wired site violate its own constitution.

# VERDICTS

**FORCE RANK:**

1. **A — The Manuscript.** Wins on points, not knockout: the only vision whose moral center (evidence made spatially co-present with claims) *deepens the site's existing ethic* rather than decorating it, and the only one whose kitsch police are auditable — but it must be convicted of re-committing the client's diagnosed sin of beautiful correctness, and ships only if the margin apparatus is treated as the non-negotiable core.
2. **B — The Atmosphere.** Highest ceiling and the only real swing at magnificence, ranked second solely because its realistic degraded state is the banned gradient — a vision you must build at 100% or not at all is a vision that will ship at 60%.
3. **C — Faciebat.** The best single poem and the worst governing law: a decay metaphor that misreads the Pillow Book's central fact — old entries are not dead — and concedes visit-one beauty in a brief that demands it.

**GRAFTS:**

1. **A's margin apparatus** — proof standing beside claim at reference height is the site's ethic promoted to architecture; steal it whole.
2. **C's asymmetric decay in the log only** — the failure fades faster than its lesson: confine the ink-age mechanism to the ledger, where recency *is* meaning, and the category error vanishes.
3. **B's hour-keyed structure without the painted sky** — which list stands open, which epigraph presides, chosen server-side by Shōnagon's clock: all the aliveness, none of the wash budget.
4. **The daylight CV** (B's evaporating sky / C's stone, independently converged — trust convergence): one page outside the night, outside time, print-canonical.
5. **The signed source** — colophon model-card, dated re-ink comment, "ruled by hand": the second reader finds the page *more* composed underneath.

**THE MISS:**

All three visions map the athlete facets as metaphors legible only in the design rationale — bar-path-as-baseline-grid, gloss-as-saucer-pass — and so all three produce a site that is *weightless*. More telling: all three mistake the touchstones for surface treatments. Turner becomes washes, Rodin becomes non-finito, Shōnagon becomes lists. But Turner's actual subject is the **sublime** — the viewer made small before something vast — and Rodin's is **mass**: scale and force felt in the body. None of the three ever makes the reader feel anything *physically*. Every vision caps the site at one display-scale line and reading-size everything else; every vision zeroes out motion and then forgets that one motion remains that no designer can remove — **the reader's own scroll** — and none of them choreographs it.

The idea none of them had: **a moment of monumental scale that the reader must travel through.** One surface — the corpus, or a single list — set at architectural size, sentences at 30vh, a real distance, paced like Rodin's *Balzac* approached on foot: the scroll as the site's only animation, the reader's hand as the only easing curve, effort and arrival as the design's payload. This is where hockey and strongman live not as captions but as felt experience — mass, distance, force — and it is the only device in reach that produces *magnificence* rather than loveliness on visit one while costing visits two through ten nothing. All three visions are beautiful at 16 pixels. The man was a strongman. Nobody built anything heavy.
