# THE LONG SECTION

**Epigraph:** *Nothing is discarded. It is only buried at the depth it deserves.*

## THE IDEA
The site is a single archaeological section drawing — the elevation made when you cut a trench and ink the exposed wall — as one continuous horizontal scroll, hand-line on permatrace grey, graphite plus one conventional red for annotation. Land on it and you see a thin strip: topsoil, scale bar, a north arrow that always points right, toward "now." Scroll right and you move along the exposed face — ground thickens and dips, older material sitting lower and further back, time read as depth-at-a-distance, not depth-underfoot. In sixty seconds you understand you're not browsing a menu. You're reading a wall.

## HOW THE LISTS LIVE IN IT
The 8 Shonagon lists are marker horizons — thin, distinct bands (a lime-wash seam, a charcoal lens) running the entire length, present at every era because a disposition doesn't stop recurring when a decade ends. Each one-line entry is a find: a drawn sherd inside its horizon at the point in the wall where it happened, tagged with a leader line to a numbered caption — the existing zero-JS disclosure, now a museum find-tag. Prose untouched; only its address changes, from "list item" to "context 1997.14, sq. C6."

## THE SIGNATURE MOMENT
Every find carries a date, not all drawn alike. A shipped repo, a published poem, a competition result — anything with primary evidence — gets a tight calibrated date, inked firm. A childhood memory with no receipt gets drawn like a radiocarbon date: a year with error bars, a soft curve instead of a hard line. The wall admits, in its own linework, which parts of the life it can prove.

## THE FACETS
Six intrusions cut the stratigraphy, each in the hand a real site-recorder uses for that material — no icons:
- Hockey — a compacted ice-lens, a vertical post-cut driven straight through it.
- Strongman — a scorched patch, heat-affected soil, hachured differently from its surrounds.
- Novelist — a robber trench: stone quarried out, the void backfilled with mixed rubble. Fiction fills the hole where the true thing used to be.
- Poet — a votive deposit: one small object placed on purpose, meant to be found later.
- AI researcher — a palimpsest surface, recut and re-inscribed by each successive occupation.
- Engineer — the one wall still standing unexcavated, in situ, load-bearing, shoring up everything beside it.

## WHY ART, NOT GIMMICK
A section drawing is a technology for honesty: nothing is placed except in provable relation to what's above, below, and beside it. Applied to a life, the form refuses the highlight reel — lower layers stay structurally present under what's built on top; a self is sequence and superposition, not a curated grid of achievements. A real trench rewards a slow, trained eye, so the site rewards re-scrolling — the tenth visit, you notice the thin lens you walked past nine times, the way a tenth reading of Shonagon turns up a different resonance.

## THE RISK
Kitsch failure: theme-park archaeology — cartoon trowels, "dig here," dirt-brush sound effects. The governing rule: nothing appears that a real site illustrator would not have drawn. If it isn't graphite, permatrace-grey, or the one conventional red — if it isn't a mark an actual recording convention produces — it doesn't go on.
