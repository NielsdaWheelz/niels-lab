# COMPULSORY FIGURES

**Epigraph:** *The judges never asked what you meant. They knelt and read the ice.*

## THE IDEA

Before free skating, competitive skating was judged on **school figures**: hours spent tracing perfect circles and eights on virgin ice, no audience, graded afterward purely on the cut left behind. The site is an aerial rink — cool survey-blue, ruled like a technical drawing — with one unbroken blade-groove running across it, begun at birth, top-left. There is no scroll bar in the usual sense; the visitor pans a flat plane in two directions, following the cut wherever it goes. And it does not go straight: it loops, doubles back, tightens into figure eights around old obsessions, crosses its own earlier passes. Distance traveled is time lived, but the *shape* of that travel — recursive, returning, sometimes retracing the same circle for years — is the actual record of a life, not a timeline's polite fiction of one.

## HOW THE LISTS LIVE IN IT

The eight Shonagon lists are eight literal **compulsory figures** — fixed loops and eights cut as spurs off the main trace, exactly where a skater would be required to demonstrate one before continuing. Panning into a loop reveals its ~60 entries etched around the rim, one per short arc. Each arc is still wired to primary evidence: tap it and a `<details>` opens as a hairline crack in the ice, flush with the surface, revealing the underlying document — zero JS beyond that native disclosure. Reading a list means skating its circle to close, arriving back at your own entry point, the way a figure is only scored complete when the tracing meets itself.

## ONE SIGNATURE MOMENT

Every stretch of ice already passed hardens to a black, mirror-cut gloss and begins faintly reflecting a warped, blurred image of your own cursor back at you — the ice remembering being skated. Ahead of wherever you currently are, the surface is still raw, unglazed white, kicked with fine spray. The site is a rink you skate by panning, and everywhere you've already been has turned into dark ice that watches you.

## WHY ART, NOT GIMMICK

Compulsory figures were mostly private labor, graded only by the mark left, never by an account of intention — which is exactly what this record is: training and drafts and research no one watched, evidenced by the cut, not the caption. That the past polishes into something that reflects your *present* cursor, distorted, is memory rendered honestly: you cannot reread your own history without the rereading changing its surface. And because the trace is a plane, not a line, no two visits need take the same route through its crossings — visit ten skates a different figure than visit one.

## THE FACETS

- **Hockey player:** the groove is not illustration — it is the site's entire structural material.
- **Strongman:** cut depth scales with force and repetition; the heaviest training years gouge the widest, darkest channel, measurable against the boards' ruled ticks.
- **Novelist:** long essays occupy the straightaways — the only stretches with no loop, because narrative is the one mode that doesn't circle back.
- **Poet:** short and /now fragments live only in the fresh, unglazed powder at the live edge, in a texture the next pass over that ground will smooth and never render the same way twice.
- **AI researcher:** projects surface as the survey annotations along the boards — coordinate ticks and legible labels, machine-readable to a crawler exactly as they are visible to an eye.
- **Engineer:** the /colophon is the boards and the plant behind them — refrigeration, resurfacing schedule, build process — construction left exposed, not hidden.

## THE RISK

Ice becomes costume: shavings, sparkle, a hockey skin worn for flavor rather than earned by mechanics. The rule: no visual effect may exist unless it corresponds to a real, verifiable property of ice under a blade — glaze from repeated passes, spray only at the live edge, depth keyed to force. Nothing decorative that isn't also documentary.
