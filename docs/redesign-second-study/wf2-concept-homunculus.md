# CANDIDATES

1. The site as a Zamboni's nightly resurfacing of a rink that is never scraped clean, only reflooded — every cut ever made lies frozen in strata beneath the current sheet, drillable like an ice core.
2. The site as a taxidermist's back room, not the mounted animals on display but the skins still salted and stretched on forms not yet decided — a life kept perpetually mid-preparation rather than finished.
3. The site as a hospital discharge folder — lists as intake forms, essays as an attending's dictated notes, the homunculus a night nurse charting vitals no one but the patient will read.
4. The site as a curling stone mid-slide across one long sheet, its line bent by the reader's scroll ("sweeping"), coming to rest in a different house each visit depending on how hard you read.
5. The site as a cutman's kit between rounds — swabs, adrenaline, gauze in labeled tins — the lists are what's stocked in each one, reordered after every bout by hands never shown.

# UNDERCUT

**Nothing here is deleted. It is only frozen over.**

## THE IDEA

The site is a sheet of arena ice, viewed from directly above, rendered as a pale plane with a faint blue undertone. Nothing moves when you land — no shader, no particles. What you notice after a few seconds is texture: fine parallel scores across the surface, like the ice after a period of play, catching light at an angle. These are the lists. You are not reading a page; you are reading what a blade left behind.

Scrolling doesn't move a camera — it changes your angle of light across the same fixed sheet, the way raking light makes ice grooves legible or invisible depending on where you stand. Text you couldn't see at the top of the page resolves as you pass it. Nothing was hidden. You just weren't standing in the right place yet.

## HOW THE LISTS LIVE IN IT

The eight lists are eight cut-lanes radiating from a center-ice dot, each a slightly different depth in the sheet. A list edited this month sits nearest the surface — crisp, high-contrast. A list that hasn't changed in years sits a layer down, faintly frosted, still fully legible but visibly *older ice*. This isn't a decorative gradient; it's literal: each list's CSS opacity and blur are derived from its last-edit timestamp, nothing hand-tuned. Depth is evidence.

## ONE SIGNATURE MOMENT

Under the center-ice dot sits a single control: a vertical slider, unlabeled except for a hairline tick per night the site has existed. Drag it down and you cut a **core sample** straight through the sheet — every past version of whatever list you're reading, stacked in frozen cross-section, most recent on top, oldest gone milky and dim at the bottom, exactly like a drilled ice core or a tree's rings turned sideways. You watch, in one gesture, every conviction he's cut and then let the water close back over. This is what someone tells a friend: *you can drill down through his opinions like ice cores and watch him change his mind in real time.*

## WHY ART, NOT GIMMICK

The image says something specific and true: this is a man who has been five different people in sequence — player, lifter, novelist, poet, researcher — and none of those selves got deleted when the next one arrived. They went dim and cold under a new layer, but the core sample proves they're still down there, unedited, drillable. That is not a metaphor bolted onto a bio; it *is* the honest shape of a multi-self record. Visit ten still rewards you because the ice is objectively, measurably thicker than it was on visit one — real timestamps, not a loop.

## THE FACETS

- **Hockey** — the medium itself is the rink; no icon needed, the whole page *is* the ice.
- **Strongman** — the core-sample slider annotates each layer's thickness in kilograms of load-bearing ice, an obsessive, literal unit no one but a strongman would think to compute.
- **Novelist** — /writing sits at the deepest, thickest-frozen stratum: the ice that took the most nights to accumulate over it.
- **Poet** — line breaks in verse follow the arc geometry of an actual skate stride, not a margin.
- **AI researcher** — the nightly resurfacing pass is logged, verbatim, as a checkpoint diff — real ML practice, not borrowed vocabulary.
- **Engineer** — the core-sample drag is git blame made physical: hover a stratum, get its real commit and timestamp.

## THE RISK

Failure mode: a WebGL frost demo, all glinting shader, no signal — or ML jargon stacked on just to claim the facet. **The rule: no layer of ice may be rendered, only revealed.** If a night produced no real edit, nothing on the page is allowed to change.
