# Electronic Literature, Poetic Computing, Net Art — Research for nielseriknandal.com

## WORKS

- **afternoon, a story** (Michael Joyce, 1987) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afternoon,_a_story — "Words that yield": links are unmarked; the reader presses on words to see which give way. "Guard fields" keep certain passages locked until others have been visited — the text remembers you, and rereading is a different reading.
- **Patchwork Girl** (Shelley Jackson, 1995) — https://www.eastgate.com/catalog/PatchworkGirl.html — Navigation is an anatomical map: a stitched female body on the left, lexias mapped to its parts on the right. "You can resurrect me, but only piecemeal… you will have to sew me together yourself." Scars and sutures are the metaphor for link and lexia. Her later *my body — a Wunderkammer* does the same autobiographically.
- **My Boyfriend Came Back From the War** (Olia Lialina, 1996) — https://anthology.rhizome.org/my-boyfriend-came-back-from-the-war — Each click subdivides the browser into smaller HTML frames; conversation fractures spatially until the screen is a mosaic of empty black cells. Layout *is* the emotional arc.
- **Agatha Appears** (Olia Lialina, 1997) — https://artbase.rhizome.org/wiki/Q1615 — Story told through link `title` attributes, the status bar, and the URL itself; character and reader "teleport" from server to server. Browser chrome as narrative surface.
- **wwwwwwwww.jodi.org** (JODI, 1995) — https://wwwwwwwww.jodi.org — Rendered page is green noise; View Source reveals meticulous ASCII diagrams of a hydrogen bomb. The source code is the artwork; the render is the decoy.
- **Dakota** (Young-Hae Chang Heavy Industries, 2002) — https://directory.eliterature.org/individual-work/4835 — Text flashed to Art Blakey's drumming; no scroll, no pause, no control. The work owns tempo; the reader submits.
- **Taper** (Bad Quarto / Trope Tank) — https://taper.badquar.to — Twice-yearly magazine of computational poems ≤2KB of HTML/CSS/JS. The byte limit is the sonnet form; constraint generates invention.
- **the html review** — https://thehtml.review — Annual journal of literature made *for* the browser. Issue exemplars: *Anonymous Animal* (Everest Pipkin; hourly synchronized durational piece — all visitors see the same moment), *A Small Memorial* (Todd Anderson; requires several simultaneous visitors pressing keys), *Cursor Park* (a resting place for your cursor), *Accumulation* (Katy Gero; a text that gathers as list-logic).
- **Taroko Gorge** (Nick Montfort, 2009) — https://nickm.com/poems/ — Endless nature poem generated a line every ~1.2s from a tiny authored vocabulary; famously remixed by 20+ poets because the code is the form. With *Sea and Spar Between* (Montfort/Strickland): a sea of stanzas the size of the ocean, navigated by coordinates.
- **…and by islands I mean paragraphs** (J.R. Carpenter, 2013) — https://luckysoap.com/andbyislands — A white sea extending beyond the viewport, dotted with islands that are generated paragraphs (corpus: *The Tempest*, *Robinson Crusoe*, Thule). Topos = place = topic; the map is the table of contents.
- **With Those We Love Alive** (Porpentine, 2014) — https://xrafstar.monster/games/twine/wtwla/ — Twine hypertext that asks the reader to draw sigils on their own skin; color-coded links distinguish "navigate" from "transform this word." The work exits the screen onto the body.
- **P2P Folder Poetry** (Melanie Hoff / SFPC) — https://lovelanguages.melaniehoff.com/folder-poetry-2021/ — Directory nesting as poetic form like haiku or iambic pentameter: file paths that scan as lines, folders that unfold as narrative.
- **ASCII History of Moving Images** (Vuk Ćosić, 1998) — http://www.ljudmila.org/~vuk/ascii/film/ — Cinema classics re-rendered as green ASCII. Radical material honesty: the character grid as chosen palette, not poverty.
- **Hypertext Gardens** (Mark Bernstein, 1998) — https://www.eastgate.com/garden/Enter.html — The garden as hypertext rhetoric: formal gateways, then deliberate, gentle irregularity between farmland (rigid nav) and wilderness (disorientation).
- **Andy Matuschak's notes** — https://notes.andymatuschak.org — Stacked sliding panes: following a link pushes a new pane beside the old; your path of thought stays visible. Hypertext that preserves provenance.
- **My website is a shifting house…** (Laurel Schwulst, 2018) — https://thecreativeindependent.com/essays/laurel-schwulst-my-website-is-a-shifting-house-next-to-a-river-of-knowledge-what-could-yours-be/ — The governing essay: a website as room, garden, puddle — a living temporal space, author as architect.
- **UbuWeb** (Kenneth Goldsmith, 1996–) — https://www.ubu.com — Thirty years of plain HTML written in a text editor, red-and-black on white. Proof that austerity can be a monument — but note it's austerity *housing* an avant-garde, which is the charge Niels's current site lacks.

## MECHANISMS TRANSFERABLE TO A PERSONAL SITE

1. **Words that yield.** In essays and lists, let certain phrases press open — an em-dash aside, a marginal gloss, a citation to primary evidence — without link-blue. The prose stays byte-sacred; the *behavior* is the annotation layer. Curiosity is rewarded, never required.
2. **The list that breathes.** A Shonagon list ("Things that quicken the heart") revealed one entry at a time on a slow clock — Dakota's tempo at contemplative speed — with an ever-present "show all at once" escape. Or: entries surface in an order keyed to the reader's hour ("In spring, the dawn" — the Pillow Book is already time-indexed; the site's palette and epigraph can follow the actual time of day).
3. **The accumulating list.** A list that genuinely grows over the years, timestamped, never pruned — a ledger, not a page. Digital-garden ethics: visible tending, visible age, `last-tended:` as a public fact.
4. **URL as verse line.** Folder poetry: `/lists/things-that-quicken-the-heart`, `/lists/things-that-should-be-short`. Design the path grammar so the sitemap reads as a poem and llms.txt reads as a table of contents to a book. The address bar carries meaning (Agatha) at zero cost to machines.
5. **Source as second text.** The HTML itself — comments, element names, attribute order — written to be read. An agent or view-source reader finds a page *more* composed, not less: JODI's move, aligned perfectly with curl-clean legibility. Put a colophon in a comment; sign the source.
6. **The body/figure as index.** Patchwork Girl's map: one drawn figure (or one spatial map of a life — rink, barbell, desk, page) whose regions are the nav for CV, writing, research, lifting. The whole person assembled piecemeal by the reader.
7. **Subdivision as time.** Lialina's frames: a page whose layout enacts its subject — a hockey career told in periods that split the viewport; a memoir column that narrows as it descends. Layout changes as rhetoric, once, where the content earns it.
8. **A sea beyond the viewport.** One wing of the site (not the CV) as a 2D field: entries as islands you sail between with arrow keys, Carpenter-style. Spatial hypertext for the lists; conventional scroll everywhere else.
9. **Stacked-pane provenance.** For interlinked writeups: links open beside, not instead of — the reader's path of thought remains on screen. Matuschak's panes are the rare hypertext UI that *adds* orientation.
10. **The 2KB room.** Adopt Taper's constraint for a corner: tiny self-contained poem-pages, each under 2KB, each doing one thing. Constraint is the anti-slop guarantee — nothing decorative fits.
11. **Presence, lightly.** One page that knows others are there — a shared counter of tonight's readers, a cursor that leaves a day-long trace. Anonymous Animal's move: communal time, not gamified chat.
12. **The garden gate.** Bernstein's rhetoric for the whole: a formal entrance (clear, machine-legible front door), then gentle irregularity inside — paths that curve, vistas that open. Not a maze; a garden.

## TRAPS

- **Hijacked physics.** Scrolljacking, cursor replacement, forced tempo on functional pages. Dakota works because it is *only* a performance; a CV that withholds is hostile. Reserve tempo-control for pieces framed as pieces.
- **Mystery navigation everywhere.** Words-that-yield is fiction's privilege. Wayfinding pages need honest links; confine concealment to one wing, and always provide the "show everything" valve.
- **Generative slop.** Taroko Gorge succeeds because Montfort authored every word of a tiny corpus and the *system* is the poem. Recombining prose you didn't weigh word-by-word violates the byte-sacred rule. If generation appears, the vocabulary is written like verse.
- **Nostalgia cosplay.** Fake Geocities chrome, starfields, ironic 90s kitsch — retro-web pastiche is AI-slop's cousin. Steal the *materiality* of net.art, never its costume.
- **JS-only meaning.** Any mechanism that makes text invisible to curl breaks the machine-surface covenant. The plain HTML must be the complete, beautiful ground truth; behavior is progressive enhancement on top.
- **The carnival page.** E-lit works are monomaniacal — one mechanism, fully committed. Five tricks on one page is a demo reel. One mechanism per room; silence elsewhere.
- **Interaction as toll-booth.** Never gate approved prose so it can't be read whole, copied, printed. The reader sews Patchwork Girl together by choice; she is never held hostage.
- **Whimsy without gravity.** Cursor parks and breathing lists turn twee unless anchored to the site's actual subjects — strength, literature, code. Every mechanism must be a metaphor for something true about the man, or it's decoration masquerading as art.
