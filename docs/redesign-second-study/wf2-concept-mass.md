# BEARING
*Everything below bears everything above.*

## THE IDEA

The site is a load-bearing wall. Every element has mass — measured, not asserted — and every line of type carries the accumulated load of the document above it. At build time, a published function assigns each element its setting from real quantities: bytes above, evidence attached, age. Newsreader and Quattro are variable fonts; their weight axis becomes structure. A page opens light at the top (wght ~380, generous leading) and settles, course by course, toward a stout, tight-leaded foundation (~720) at the footer — which states the truth like plates counted aloud: *This page weighs 38,214 bytes and bears seven years.* In the first sixty seconds the visitor reads a beautiful austere page, scrolls, and feels — before seeing — that the type is settling under them. Nothing moves. Nothing performs. The page simply weighs something.

## HOW THE LISTS LIVE IN IT

Each Shonagon list is one wall. Each byte-sacred entry is a course of masonry: full-measure line, hairline mortar joint beneath. The zero-JS disclosures become shoring — closed, the claim rests lightly on its joint; opened, the primary evidence appears as a recessed pier set in the *heaviest* type on the page. The semantic inversion is the whole ethic: assertion is light, evidence is dense, because evidence is what actually holds the claim up.

## ONE SIGNATURE MOMENT

The /log. Seven years in one column, and the gradient runs across years: this week's entries breathe at 380; scroll to 2019 and the type has settled to near-slab — tight, dark, compressed by everything stacked on it since. What you tell a friend: "His past is literally denser. The old entries are load-bearing."

## WHY ART, NOT GIMMICK

The form states one true thing a strongman knows in his spine: load is the most honest fact about a body, and a life's record works the same way — the past bears the present. It survives visit ten because it is subliminal, like meter in verse: no animation, no scroll tricks, a gradient tuned to be felt before it is noticed, rewarding attention rather than exhausting it. And it costs machines nothing — the HTML is clean semantic lists; curl sees Shonagon, unstyled and perfect. Cezanne's method: weight rendered by structure, never by outline.

## THE FACETS

- **Hockey player** — /now is ice: the one unloaded page, lightest setting on the site, widest leading, present tense gliding frictionless. Nothing has settled yet.
- **Strongman** — every footer declares true tonnage: actual bytes served, no compression lies, the honest count before the pull.
- **Novelist** — essays obey narrative gravity: the gradient maps to the arc, and the final paragraph of every essay is set heaviest, bearing the whole story.
- **Poet** — verse and quotation are exempt from load: they hang from above, light, suspended — poems are the only things on the site that do not stack.
- **AI researcher** — the colophon publishes a model card for the typography: weight(element) = f(depth, bytes, evidence, age), inputs and outputs tabulated. "Weights," meant literally, both ways.
- **Engineer** — the calculations are real and inspectable: view-source shows each course annotated with its computed load; the build script is linked from the colophon.

## THE RISK

The kitsch mode: physics toy — scroll-triggered boldening, black illegible slabs, strain *performed* instead of borne. The single policing rule: **no element may display a weight it was not computed to carry.** Every value derives from measured quantities via the published function, and the whole gradient lives inside legibility. If you notice the mechanism before you feel it, the setting is wrong.
