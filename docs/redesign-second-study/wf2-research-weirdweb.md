# THE WEIRD WEB — Design Research

## EXEMPLARS

**Bloomberg.com (2016 redesign)** — bloomberg.com — Reclaims default hyperlink-blue on black-and-white as a *material*, not an accident. Brutalism here means using the web's own factory-issue elements deliberately instead of styling them away.

**Craigslist** — craigslist.org — Thirty-one years with no redesign. The refusal to modernize *is* the design statement: truth to function over truth to trend.

**Brutalist Websites (Pascal Deville's canon)** — brutalistwebsites.com — The curatorial act itself is the mechanism. Naming an anti-aesthetic and archiving examples turned "sites that don't try to look nice" into a legible movement with critical vocabulary.

**Cameron's World** — cameronsworld.net — A collage of 700+ GIFs scavenged from dead GeoCities pages. Density reads as sincerity, not chaos, because every fragment is sourced archaeology, not generated decoration — a love letter assembled from real wreckage.

**JODI** — wwwwwwwww.jodi.org — Pages that leak ASCII, spawn windows, and simulate viral behavior. Critics call it "faux-code," "faux-virus" — the hostility is theater aimed at a specific target: the web's shift from "window-breaking" to "curtain-making," i.e. commercial smoothness. Broken-on-purpose, at something.

**fr-08: .the .product (Farbrausch)** — pouet.net/prod.php?which=1221 — An entire 11-minute 3D/audio show inside 64 kilobytes, with textures, geometry, and music generated procedurally at runtime — nothing stored, everything earned by code. The constraint isn't a limitation worked around; it's the entire artistic proposition.

**Dwitter** — dwitter.net — Visual JS demos capped at 140 characters. The hard number functions like a sonnet's meter: a fixed constraint that forces invention rather than suppressing it.

**Low-Tech Magazine, solar edition** — solar.lowtechmagazine.com — Runs on a solar-powered server in Barcelona; goes offline in bad weather; images are dithered to four shades of gray via custom compression. The site is honest about its own physical limits and lets that honesty become the visual signature (grainy, sketch-like images) rather than hiding it behind a CDN.

**Zombo.com** — zombo.com — One looping Flash animation, one joke ("You can do anything"), sustained without a single wink of escalation since 1999. It outlives its punchline through absolute, humorless commitment to the bit.

**Yesterweb / Web Revival + webrings** — yesterweb.org — Personal homepages linked hand-to-hand via webrings instead of discovered through a feed. Navigation itself becomes an authored, anti-algorithmic gesture — you are pointed somewhere by a person, not a ranking.

## STEALABLE MOVES

1. **Truth to materials.** Expose the actual medium instead of disguising it — default link-blue, visible monospace, plain borders, real HTML semantics. For a literary site: don't hide the manuscript's seams; let markup read as manuscript.

2. **Constraint as form, stated aloud.** 64k intros, Dwitter's 140 characters, js1k's 1024 bytes — each community makes the number the headline, not a hidden implementation detail. A byte-budget or single-typeface rule, named in the colophon as content, functions like a sonnet declaring its own meter.

3. **Refusal as statement.** Craigslist's non-redesign and Gemini's no-JS-no-tracking stance are arguments, not laziness. The zero-JS disclosures already in the current site should be declared, not apologized for — "this page contains no script" as a line of prose, not a technical footnote.

4. **Density as sincerity, never as noise.** Cameron's World's maximalism works only because each fragment is real, evidenced, found. This maps directly onto the Shonagon lists: the pile-up of entries earns its density exactly because every line is wired to primary evidence — the same rule that makes maximalism art instead of clutter.

5. **Hostility needs a target.** JODI's breakage critiques something specific (commercial polish). Any deliberate friction on a redesign — a slow reveal, an uncomfortable pause, a refusal to summarize — must be aimed at a named enemy (algorithmic skimming, infinite scroll, AI-tell smoothness), or it just reads as a bug.

6. **Show the site's own physical state.** Low-Tech Magazine displays battery charge and goes dark in storms. The equivalent here: expose real operational facts already tracked in /log and /now — last-commit timestamps, word counts, a training log entry from that morning — as visible material, not metadata buried in source.

7. **Curation is authorship.** Brutalistwebsites.com's real work is the naming and framing, not any one linked site. The eight Shonagon lists already do this — the category title is itself the artistic claim, before any entry is read.

8. **Anti-algorithmic wayfinding.** Webrings replace "related posts" with a person's hand-picked next link. Apply this to /projects or /writing: a single curated "read next" chosen by the author, never a recommendation engine's guess.

9. **Sustain the bit without a wink.** Zombo.com and JS1k demos succeed by never breaking character to signal "isn't this clever." An austere, correct-but-beautiful site should hold its tone dead level — no self-aware irony undercutting the seriousness, even in playful moments.

## WHERE WEIRD BECOMES SLOP

**When the constraint is decorative, not load-bearing.** Vaporwave curdled once palm trees and VHS artifacts became wallpaper applied over a template rather than a genuine formal limit — the demoscene equivalent of faking a 64k intro's look in a 10MB page. A byte-budget or single-typeface rule must actually bind the build, or it's costume.

**When hostility has no target.** JODI's breakage indicts something; a site that's merely hard to use, with no named enemy, just reads as broken — the gap between "faux-virus" as critique and an unstyled form that's simply an accident.

**When maximalism is generated instead of gathered.** Cameron's World's power is evidentiary — real scavenged GIFs, not synthetic decoration. This is the exact line Niels already draws against AI-tell design: density built from primary sources reads as devotion; density built from stock or generated filler reads as slop, full stop.

**When nostalgia becomes cosplay.** Imitating Web 1.0's surface — tables, marquee tags, garish color — without its underlying ethos (self-published, non-commercial, untracked) is costume, not lineage. The Gemini/small-web movement earns its rawness because the constraint serves a stated value (no tracking, no ads); borrowing the look without the value is kitsch.

**When the gimmick doesn't survive a second visit.** Js1k demos are built for a single "whoa" inside a competition frame. A personal site meant to be read, cited, and returned to for years needs its strangeness to still function on the hundredth visit — durability, not shock, is the test a literary site must pass that a demo never has to.
