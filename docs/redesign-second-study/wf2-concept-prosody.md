# CANDIDATES

1. **Ice core** — the site is a drilled core sample from a frozen rink, scrolled top (present) to bottom (childhood), banded by year, thickness proportional to how much that year compressed into it.
2. **Scoresheet** — the entire site is one continuously-kept hockey scorer's ledger; essays, log, and CV are just different columns of the same monospace game sheet, periods instead of pages.
3. **Loaded bar** — the homepage is a barbell; each of the 8 lists is a plate you load on by hand, and the running total becomes the day's epigraph, a life expressed as tonnage.
4. **Zamboni pass** — each visit resurfaces the ice fresh; your reading path leaves a faint blade groove across the page that fades by your next visit, so the site remembers nothing on purpose.
5. **Center ice** — the whole document is a regulation rink seen from above; scrolling is skating the neutral zone, and the 8 lists live at the 8 real rink landmarks — blue lines, dots, crease, boards.

## NAME + EPIGRAPH

**CORE**
*Nothing is lost. It is only compressed.*

## THE IDEA

The site is a vertical bore hole through an ice core pulled from a frozen rink. A tall strip runs down the center of the viewport — real glaciology-photo convention — banded into horizontal layers, one per year, palest and thinnest at the top. Scrolling *is* drilling down: present at the surface, 1994 near the bottom. In the first sixty seconds a visitor lands in this year's thin new ice, sees the band widths vary wildly below (some years dense and dark, some almost air), and clicks one — it splits open sideways into a lit "thin section," the format glaciologists use to read a slice under a scope, showing that year's actual content.

## HOW THE LISTS LIVE IN IT

The 8 lists aren't navigation, they're the 8 named horizons that recur at every depth of a real core: hockey, strongman, novel, poems, research, code. Each list's ~60 entries render as flecks trapped in the ice of every year they touched — click a fleck, it melts, and the primary-evidence citation beads out beneath it. The log ledger *is* the banding itself, accreting in real time at the top. Essays are melt events — a warmer year, clearer ice — rendered in full Newsreader while everything else stays compressed monospace noise until opened. Projects are ash layers, foreign matter frozen mid-motion. The dense print CV becomes the corelog table proper — depth, age, event — barely touched, just correctly labeled. The colophon becomes materials-and-methods: the drill, the bit, the fonts.

## ONE SIGNATURE MOMENT

A fixed monospace depth gauge in the margin ticks as you scroll, converting pixel position to calendar year against real calibration points from his own record — marker horizons, in the drilling term — so at some point it simply reads: *"1994 — age 6, first skates."* Scrolling performs actual ice-core dating on a human life. That's the sentence someone repeats to a friend.

## WHY ART, NOT GIMMICK

Compression isn't a browsing metaphor here, it's the argument: a life becomes legible *because* it was compressed, not despite it. What looks like loss — years thinned to a sliver, selves siloed into bands — is the only reason any of it can still be read at depth. It also literalizes zuihitsu itself: "jottings following the movement of the mind" is exactly how ice accretes, layer on layer, no revision of what's beneath. It survives visit ten because return visits are how you learn to read it — recognizing calibration markers, noticing a band you missed — the opposite of a trick that spends itself once.

## THE FACETS

- **Hockey player** — the medium is not a metaphor for ice, it is ice; no metaphor-within-metaphor.
- **Strongman** — band density is literally load: heavier training years compress darker, more entries per millimeter.
- **Novelist** — prose only ever renders at full serif size in melt events; elsewhere it stays compressed until earned.
- **Poet** — stanza breaks are drawn as actual fracture lines in the ice art, spaced to meter — the crack *is* the caesura.
- **AI researcher** — the depth-to-year mapping exposes its own interpolation curve as a small sparkline — the one place the machine shows its work.
- **Engineer** — every band is generated from ledger data, not drawn; view-source shows the deterministic function, curl-clean.

## THE RISK

Kitsch failure: cracking-ice sound effects, cursor frost, parallax snow — a novelty rink site. The rule: no visual property of the ice exists unless it is first true as data. If a fracture, fleck, or band's thickness can't be traced to one literal logged number, it does not render.
