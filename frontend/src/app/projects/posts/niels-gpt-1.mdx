niels-gpt-1 is a byte-level GPT, written and trained from scratch on a
MacBook Air. It came out of
[makemore](https://github.com/NielsdaWheelz/makemore/blob/fb72b74d63d29a75cc084d876990d4904642fba1/README.md),
two November days of Karpathy's zero-to-hero exercises: character-level
name models and a hand-written autograd class.

The
[charter](https://github.com/NielsdaWheelz/niels-gpt-1/blob/257190b3c136f70a26c5ae43dd732bd634582269/docs/charter.md)
fixes the goal and the box — "i write the transformer code, i train the
weights, and i can explain every part"; one M4 laptop on MPS; a training run
under six hours; a byte-level tokenizer. Two days separate the first commit,
2025-12-14, from the
[published checkpoint](https://huggingface.co/nnandal/niels-gpt), whose
`best.pt` is dated 2025-12-16.

The tokenizer was the hard call. Subwords from day one were the alternative;
the charter pins
[bytes](https://github.com/NielsdaWheelz/niels-gpt-1/blob/257190b3c136f70a26c5ae43dd732bd634582269/niels_gpt/tokenizer.py)
— vocab 256, every byte a token. A tokenizer I did not build would have been
the one part I could not explain. The cost is context: T=384
tokens is 384 bytes, roughly a hundred words of memory.

The rest is deliberately legible.
[RoPE implemented by hand](https://github.com/NielsdaWheelz/niels-gpt-1/commit/356d4d7757956509a98ebf524d98c7f315cd9f82),
with its own PR and tests. A decoder-only transformer with LayerNorm, GELU
MLP, and weight tying, at
[V=256, T=384, C=384, six layers, four heads](https://github.com/NielsdaWheelz/niels-gpt-1/blob/257190b3c136f70a26c5ae43dd732bd634582269/niels_gpt/config.py).
Pretraining mixes wikitext-103, my Roam notes, and a hand-written primer at
0.72, 0.18, and 0.10. A
[chat CLI](https://github.com/NielsdaWheelz/niels-gpt-1/blob/257190b3c136f70a26c5ae43dd732bd634582269/niels_gpt/chat_cli.py)
with temperature and top-k sampling and stop sequences. Attention stays a
manual softmax, and a
[traced forward path](https://github.com/NielsdaWheelz/niels-gpt-1/commit/629aa061becac3e19fef7fd6b212e17f0395f2e3)
returns the probability rows
[niels-gpt-app](/projects/niels-gpt-app) displays.

Numbers, by wc -l and grep over tracked files: 5,720 lines of Python across
43 files; 107 test functions in
[16 files](https://github.com/NielsdaWheelz/niels-gpt-1/tree/257190b3c136f70a26c5ae43dd732bd634582269/tests);
the dependencies are torch and datasets.

What is wrong with it. No loss curve, no eval number, and no sample
transcript is committed anywhere, so I can cite the machinery and not the
model. The README's caveats contradict the config in one place — "context is
512 tokens" against a T=384 default. Best-checkpoint selection keys on
wikitext loss alone. And the repo is a frozen snapshot: the original repo
was renamed and rewritten in place as niels-gpt-2, the two share their first
29 commits, and v1's
[final commit](https://github.com/NielsdaWheelz/niels-gpt-1/commit/257190b3c136f70a26c5ae43dd732bd634582269)
is a README todo recording the link debt the split created.

The byte-level constraint was the point.
[niels-gpt-2](/projects/niels-gpt-2) removes it.
